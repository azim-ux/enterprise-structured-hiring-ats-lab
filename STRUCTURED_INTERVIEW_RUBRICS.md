# Structured Interview Rubrics

## Administration protocol

Use the same core question and permitted probes for every candidate in a requisition. Capture the situation, candidate action, rationale, result, and learning. Score only observable evidence. Each assessor locks four ratings before calibration; demographic data, source channel, and other assessors’ scores remain hidden until the lock.

At enterprise volume, the ATS assigns an assessment-kit version, randomized trained assessor, capacity window, and evidence-note quality check to every panel. Questions remain equivalent within a requisition family; G1 prompts permit education, training, community, or personal-project examples so prior corporate access does not become an unintended gate. Assessor dashboards monitor completion, severity, central tendency, and excessive score changes without exposing demographic cohorts.

Overall structured interview score = `(competency 1 + competency 2 + competency 3 + competency 4) / 4`.

## 1. Technical Problem Solving & Quality Mindset

**Core question:** Tell us about a technically complex defect or service failure you diagnosed. How did you protect the customer, establish root cause, and verify the correction?

**Permitted probes:** What evidence changed your hypothesis? What did you measure? What would have happened if you had done nothing?

| Rating | Behavioral anchor |
|---:|---|
| **1 — Unsatisfactory** | Jumps to a preferred answer; cannot distinguish symptom from cause; ignores containment, safety, quality, or verification. |
| **2 — Developing** | Describes a plausible fix but uses limited evidence, weak prioritization, or incomplete validation. |
| **3 — Proficient** | Frames the problem, protects immediate stakeholders, tests evidence-based causes, implements a proportionate correction, and verifies results. |
| **4 — Advanced** | Integrates multiple data sources, quantifies risk, challenges assumptions, and builds prevention into the process. |
| **5 — Role Model** | Creates a reusable diagnostic approach, surfaces system causes, balances safety and delivery transparently, and demonstrates sustained prevention with independent evidence. |

## 2. Stakeholder Collaboration & Conflict Resolution

**Core question:** Describe a disagreement with a stakeholder over a priority, standard, or decision. How did you reach a workable outcome?

**Permitted probes:** What did the other party need? What did you change in your own approach? How was the agreement recorded?

| Rating | Behavioral anchor |
|---:|---|
| **1 — Unsatisfactory** | Blames others, avoids the conflict, relies on authority, or cannot describe an outcome. |
| **2 — Developing** | Communicates a position but explores stakeholder needs superficially or leaves ownership unclear. |
| **3 — Proficient** | Clarifies interests, uses relevant evidence, listens actively, agrees owners and dates, and preserves a constructive working relationship. |
| **4 — Advanced** | Reframes competing interests around shared outcomes, adapts communication, and resolves underlying process friction. |
| **5 — Role Model** | Enables durable cross-functional trust, creates a fair decision mechanism, and shares learning that prevents similar conflict across teams. |

## 3. Process Optimization & Continuous Improvement

**Core question:** Walk us through a process you improved. How did you choose the problem, test the change, and confirm benefit?

**Permitted probes:** What was the baseline? Who experienced the process? What trade-off or unintended effect did you monitor?

| Rating | Behavioral anchor |
|---:|---|
| **1 — Unsatisfactory** | Offers activity without a defined problem, baseline, customer need, or evidence of improvement. |
| **2 — Developing** | Identifies waste and implements a local change but has limited measurement, control, or stakeholder involvement. |
| **3 — Proficient** | Defines baseline and target, maps the process, tests a practical change, measures results, and assigns ongoing ownership. |
| **4 — Advanced** | Uses segmented data, anticipates failure modes, balances efficiency with quality, and standardizes a successful change. |
| **5 — Role Model** | Builds a repeatable improvement system, develops others’ capability, validates sustained benefit, and openly reports negative or mixed findings. |

## 4. Adaptability & Ethical Governance

**Core question:** Tell us about a time new information or pressure forced you to change course while protecting an important standard.

**Permitted probes:** Which standard was at risk? Who did you inform? What evidence would have caused a different decision?

| Rating | Behavioral anchor |
|---:|---|
| **1 — Unsatisfactory** | Conceals risk, follows pressure without question, changes direction arbitrarily, or treats policy as someone else’s responsibility. |
| **2 — Developing** | Recognizes the issue but escalates late, documents incompletely, or proposes a change without a clear risk assessment. |
| **3 — Proficient** | Reassesses evidence, protects non-negotiable standards, consults the right owner, documents the decision, and communicates the change. |
| **4 — Advanced** | Anticipates ethical and operational consequences, creates options, and adapts quickly while preserving traceability and fairness. |
| **5 — Role Model** | Challenges unsafe incentives constructively, protects affected people, creates a stronger governance control, and makes learning visible without retaliation. |

## Evidence note standard

Write short factual notes: `context → observed action → result → anchor link`. Do not record appearance, accent, age inference, family status, health information, personality labels, or protected characteristics. “Strong executive presence” is not evidence. “Compared three defect hypotheses against measurement data and rejected two” is evidence.

## Calibration rules

1. Scores remain locked before discussion.
2. A difference of 2 or more points on a competency requires evidence review.
3. The panel may correct a rating only by citing a missed or misclassified behavior; it may not average away disagreement automatically.
4. Any post-lock change records original score, revised score, reason, approver, and timestamp.
5. The hiring manager cannot veto the composite with a subjective impression. Exceptions require People & Culture and Compliance approval and cannot bypass a failed required criterion.
6. Batch calibration reviews score distributions and rule execution, never adjusts candidates to meet a target pass rate.
7. Assessment items showing unusual exposure, response-time patterns, or assessor drift are quarantined by version; impacted candidates receive an equivalent reassessment where appropriate.
