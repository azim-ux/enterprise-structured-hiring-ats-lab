Risk and operating discipline

Human judgment remains reviewable.

  • Halo-effect review: a +0.68 subjective-versus-composite gap flags CAND-2026-0013 for review without diagnosing intent.
  • Structured rubrics: documentation specifies anchored questions, common probes, and scoring notes to limit uncontrolled variance.
  • Role-based access: proposed production RBAC separates recruiter, interviewer, manager, HRIS, and audit permissions; no runtime enforcement exists.
  • Reason codes: the proposed workflow requires documented, job-related evidence for dispositions and overrides.
  • Privacy: public data is synthetic. Retention, erasure, encryption, and field isolation are production requirements, not implemented controls.
  • UAT: 14 documented scenarios specify acceptance checks; they do not prove production readiness.
Built deliverables: synthetic datasets, dictionary, workflow and RACI, rubrics, selection-evidence rationale, fairness-monitoring matrix, privacy matrix, UAT register, dashboard, and deck.

Executive takeaway

Use evidence without confusing a model for a system.

What leaders can examine

Modeled funnel loss, requisition service levels, evidence gaps, and capacity signals.

What the artifact demonstrates

A five-requisition reference workflow, deterministic formulas, traceable records, and paginated static views.

What production teams must add

RBAC, encryption, immutable logs, retention and erasure, integrations, backups, recovery, and incident response.

What the model does not claim

No production system, real applicant data, compliance, accessibility conformance, validated fairness, or local predictive validity.

Research anchor: Sackett et al. (2022) selection-method meta-analysis, applied as an evidence foundation rather than a claim of local predictive validity.
