# Research Foundation

## Decision question

How can a small manufacturer process 4,000 applications across five job families with job-related, repeatable, reviewable selection without mistaking scale for scientific certainty?

## Evidence base

### Sackett et al. (2022)

Sackett, Zhang, Berry, and Lievens revisited meta-analytic validity estimates and argued that common range-restriction corrections had often overcorrected predictor–criterion relationships. Their revised estimates were generally lower than earlier summaries while retaining the practical value of well-designed selection methods; structured interviews ranked strongly. This lab uses the supplied corrected coefficients as design priors: structured behavioral interview **r = .42**, work sample **r = .43**, job-knowledge test **r = .40**, and unstructured interview **r = .12**.

These coefficients are not locally estimated APD effects. They justify the architecture and the explicit rejection of intuitive interviews as a decision score; they do not validate this synthetic instrument. Source: Sackett, P. R., Zhang, C., Berry, C. M., & Lievens, F. (2022), *Journal of Applied Psychology, 107*(11), 2040–2068, [doi:10.1037/apl0000994](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000994).

### Schmidt & Hunter (1998)

Schmidt and Hunter synthesized decades of personnel-selection research and connected predictive validity with expected utility. Their influential estimates preceded the revision concerns raised by Sackett et al.; therefore, this lab uses their paper to explain validity and utility concepts, not to substitute older coefficients for the newer design priors. Their discussion also supports combining complementary, job-related predictors rather than relying on a single conversation. Source: Schmidt, F. L., & Hunter, J. E. (1998), *Psychological Bulletin, 124*(2), 262–274, [doi:10.1037/0033-2909.124.2.262](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.124.2.262).

### Campion et al. (1997)

Campion, Palmer, and Campion identified 15 ways to add interview structure across content and evaluation. The operational lessons used here are: base questions on job analysis; ask candidates consistent core questions; limit unscored prompting; use behaviorally anchored scales; train interviewers; take evidence notes; score independently before discussion; and use multiple assessors. Source: Campion, M. A., Palmer, D. K., & Campion, J. E. (1997), *Personnel Psychology, 50*(3), 655–702, [doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.1997.tb00709.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.1997.tb00709.x).

## Why BARS

Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales translate a competency into observable evidence at defined performance levels. Anchors reduce ambiguity by giving assessors a shared reference, but they do not eliminate rater severity, similarity bias, halo effects, or poor probing. APD therefore combines anchors with interviewer training, independent score locking, 48-hour feedback controls, calibration, and audit logs.

The four competencies in this lab are each rated from 1 to 5. The structured-interview score is the mean of the four competency ratings. Candidate-facing accommodations change the mode or timing of an assessment where needed, never the job-related construct or scoring standard.

## Design implications

| Evidence | System response |
|---|---|
| Structured methods show stronger criterion validity than intuition | Intuition is recorded only as a diagnostic comparison and has zero composite weight |
| Work samples closely resemble job behavior | A standardized two-hour simulation receives 40% weight |
| Interviews become stronger when content and scoring are structured | Same core questions, probes, anchors, and independent scoring for every candidate in a requisition |
| Predictor validities can be overestimated | Coefficients are treated as external priors; local validation is required before production use |
| Selection quality and subgroup outcomes must be examined together | Stage-level monitoring is isolated from interviewer access and reviewed by compliance roles |

## Large-sample statistical power

The enterprise cohort contains **4,000** candidates: 2,400 Reference Group and 1,600 Focal Group applicants. Compared with the earlier demonstration cohort, this **large-sample** design gives a Chi-square independence test more statistical power to detect small differences in stage progression. That is useful for monitoring, but power is not validity. With large samples, a small operational difference may be statistically detectable while having limited practical importance; a material process failure may also remain hidden if the measured groups, stage definitions, or job-family strata are wrong.

APD therefore reports effect size and operational meaning with any significance test: group counts, selection rates, adverse-impact ratio, percentage-point difference, requisition stratum, assessment version, and confidence interval. Statistical significance never substitutes for job relevance or a root-cause review.

## Validity argument to establish before real deployment

1. **Content evidence:** subject-matter experts map every exercise and question to current critical work behaviors.
2. **Response-process evidence:** trained assessors apply identical instructions and record behavioral evidence.
3. **Internal structure:** inter-rater reliability and competency correlations are monitored without forcing unlike constructs into one score.
4. **Criterion evidence:** after an appropriate period and with privacy safeguards, scores are compared with independently defined performance criteria.
5. **Consequences:** adverse impact, accessibility, candidate reactions, false positives, and false negatives are reviewed.

## Non-causality caveat

The lab is synthetic and cross-sectional. It is **non-causal**: no observed dashboard relationship establishes that a selection method caused performance, speed, fairness, or retention. The validity coefficients originate in external meta-analysis; the APD values are constructed examples. A 0.87 impact ratio does not prove absence of discrimination, and a higher composite does not guarantee future performance.

## Research governance

Research is versioned in the ATS control register. Any change to weights, anchors, thresholds, or assessment content requires a documented rationale, subject-matter review, fairness impact assessment, UAT, and approval before release. Local evidence can refine the model only when sample size, criterion quality, and legal basis are adequate.
